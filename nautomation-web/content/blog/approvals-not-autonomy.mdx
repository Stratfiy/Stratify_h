Every agent demo ends the same way: the agent does everything, the presenter smiles, nobody asks what happens when it is wrong.

In a business, wrong has a cost. A purchase order to the wrong vendor. A quote sent with last year's price. A customer told a delivery date nobody agreed. So we build agents the other way around.

## The rule

An agent may work on its own until it is about to do one of three things:

- **Send** something outside the company: an email, a message, a call.
- **Spend** or commit money: a purchase order, a payment, a contract term.
- **Delete** or overwrite data.

At that point it stops and asks a named person. The person sees exactly what will happen, can edit it, and approves or rejects. Then the agent continues.

## "Doesn't that slow it down?"

Less than you'd think. Most of the work is before the gate: reading the thread, finding the spec, drafting the message, comparing quotes. The approval itself is a ten-second decision on a card. What you lose in seconds you gain in never having to apologise to a supplier.

And approvals shrink over time. After a few weeks the record shows which actions were always approved unchanged. Those can be moved to a lighter check, with the change itself on record.

## Why the record matters more than the approval

An approval protects you from one bad action. The record protects you from not knowing. When a customer asks "who told you that date?", the answer is a row with a time, a transcript and a name. When an auditor asks how AI is used, the answer is an export.
