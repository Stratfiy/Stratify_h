Most AI pilots fail for a boring reason: nobody agreed what success was before they started. Here is the structure we use, and why each part exists.

## The three ones

**One task.** Not "procurement". One thing: chasing purchase-order confirmations, or matching invoices. Something that happens every week and annoys someone.

**One channel.** Email, or WhatsApp, or phone. Not all three. Each channel has its own failure modes and you want to learn them one at a time.

**One team.** Two to ten people who will actually approve the agent's actions. Their reactions are the data.

## Day 0 to 30: scope and build

We write down the task, the channel, the team, the success criteria and the kill criterion. The kill criterion is the number that, if we miss it, we stop. Agreeing it up front is what keeps the pilot honest. Then we build: connect the inbox or the number, load the vendor list, set the approval rules.

## Day 31 to 60: shadow mode

The agent does the work, but every action goes through an approval. Your team sees each draft, edits or rejects, and the record fills up. This is where instructions get fixed. Expect a lot of rejections in week one and very few by week four.

## Day 61 to 90: live on one channel

The agent acts inside its limits. Send, spend and delete still ask first. The team's job shifts to a weekly review of the record.

## Day 90: review

A scorecard against the criteria from day 0. Hours saved, cycle time, error rate, and the team's own verdict. Then one of three decisions: expand to the next task, adjust and run another month, or stop. Stopping is a fine outcome. It means the criteria did what they were for.
